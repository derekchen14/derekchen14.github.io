## The Dawn of Domestic Power
In 1882, Thomas Edison established the Pearl Street Station in New York City, launching the first commercial central power grid and marking the formal beginning of the electrical age. Despite this milestone, early domestic adoption remained highly restricted, confined almost entirely to wealthy urban pockets where residents could afford the expensive new service.

For the average citizen, public hesitation was widespread, fueled by intense safety anxieties and the highly publicized, bitter battle over competing alternating current and direct current standards. These fears were far from baseless, as early domestic installations frequently relied on exposed wiring retrofitted directly through existing gas fixtures, presenting immediate fire and shock hazards to the first brave homeowners.

## Urban vs. Rural Divides

- Cities rapidly adopt electric streetlights and transit, leaving homes as a secondary priority
- Private utilities focus exclusively on high-density metropolitan areas for profitability
- Rural American homes remain entirely in the dark, relying on kerosene and manual labor
- The massive economic gap widens between electrified urban centers and isolated farming communities

## The Great Depression and the New Deal

- The creation of the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) in 1933 brings federally subsidized power
- President Franklin D. Roosevelt signs the Rural Electrification Act (REA) of 1936
- Cooperative utility models allow rural communities to build their own local grid infrastructure
- Government-backed loans accelerate transmission line construction across America's heartland

## A Transformed American Household

- Electricity shifts from a luxury novelty to an essential standard of living by the late 1930s
- Electric appliances like refrigerators, washing machines, and irons revolutionize domestic labor
- The physical layout of homes adapts to accommodate wall outlets and dedicated utility spaces
- Standardized electrical grids lay the groundwork for the modern consumer electronics boom
