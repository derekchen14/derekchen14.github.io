## intro
Ask any experienced gardener what kills the most first-year vegetable beds and you'll get the same answer. It's not pests or bad soil, but the arrival of July. The plants that were thriving in June start looking tired within days of the first real heat wave, with leaves drooping by afternoon and growth stalling. Many beginners respond by watering even more, which usually makes things worse instead of better.

## the july slump
There is a specific mechanism behind this slump, and it is easy to understand. Plants that spent May and June with easy access to surface moisture never developed deep roots. When July heat spikes water demand and dries the surface layer, those shallow roots run out of moisture, sometimes within a single hot afternoon.

## watering mistakes
A quick five-minute daily sprinkle only wets the top inch of soil, evaporating by mid-morning before it ever reaches the roots. Two long soakings a week, delivering real volume down six inches or more, beats seven light ones every time.

## heat stress signs
Wilting in the early afternoon is not automatically a sign of underwatering, as plants close their leaves partway in peak heat as a defense and often recover by evening. The signs that require attention are wilting that persists into the next morning, leaf edges turning brown and crisp, and new growth stopping entirely. These symptoms point to a root system that cannot keep up, rather than a plant that needs another light misting.

## what to do instead
Switch to two deep soakings a week instead of daily sprinkling, water in the early morning so plants enter peak heat already hydrated, and lay down two to three inches of mulch to slow evaporation and keep roots cooler. Give it two to three weeks and most struggling gardens visibly recover, since it takes that long for new deep roots to actually grow into the moisture below.
